Organizing what you eat does not require a complicated diet. Public health references, such as the Dietary Guidelines for the Brazilian Population and World Health Organization guidance, point to a few simple principles that help day to day.

## Choose real food

The Brazilian guidelines recommend basing your diet on fresh or minimally processed foods, such as rice, beans, vegetables, greens, fruit, eggs and meat. Ultra-processed products should be avoided.

## Plan your main meals

- **Think about the week.** Having a few basic meals decided cuts down on last-minute choices.
- **Build a varied plate.** Combine a protein source, a grain or tuber, and vegetables.
- **Keep practical options at hand.** Washed fruit and portions set aside in advance make better choices easier.

## Watch sugar and salt

The WHO recommends keeping free sugars below 10% of daily energy and eating less than 5 g of salt a day, just under a teaspoon. Reading labels helps you see where they show up.

## Log without guilt

Logging is there to help you see your day, not to judge your choices. Logging right after eating is usually more accurate than trying to remember at night.
