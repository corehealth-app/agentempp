Changing how you eat and live depends less on willpower and more on habits. A habit is something you end up doing almost without thinking, because it is tied to a context that repeats.

## How long it takes

A study published in 2010 by Lally and colleagues followed 96 people who repeated a simple behavior every day, always in the same context. The average time for the behavior to become automatic was 66 days, but it ranged from 18 to 254 days across participants.

In other words, there is no magic number. For some people and behaviors it takes weeks, for others, months.

## One missed day does not erase progress

In the same study, missing the behavior on a single day had little effect on habit formation. What matters is consistency over time.

## How to apply it

- **Tie the new habit to a cue.** For example, log breakfast right after you eat.
- **Start small.** An easy step you can repeat is worth more than a big goal you drop.
- **Keep the same context.** The same time and place help make it automatic.
- **If you slip, come back the next day.** No making up for it and no guilt.
